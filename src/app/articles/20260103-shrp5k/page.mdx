This morning I ran my first race of the year: the Run Houston! Sam Houston Race Park 5K. I wanted to write this one down while it's still fresh, because it felt like an important marker—not just another result, but a real starting point.

The effort was hard. Honest work from start to finish. And it ended up being my best 5K time in months, which made it even more meaningful.

The morning started simply. Cool temperatures, which is always a gift in Houston, and a familiar pre-race routine: coffee and egg bites from Starbucks before heading out. Nothing fancy. Just enough comfort and normalcy to calm the nerves.

I ran the race with my wife and a couple of friends, which made the whole experience lighter and more enjoyable. There's something grounding about lining up with people you know—less pressure, more shared energy.

For the run itself, I used the Nike Run Club app and chose the guided “5K Race” run with Coach Bennett. I love running with him in my ears. He has this perfect mix of silly and real—encouraging without being cheesy, honest without being heavy. He kept me smiling when things started to hurt and reminded me why I was out there in the first place.

I felt strong through about the halfway point. Then I hit the water station and made a decision I don't usually make—I took a roughly 40-second walk break to recover.

Normally, I'll grab two cups and keep moving, but volunteers were stretched thin and cups were disappearing as fast as they could be filled. I didn't want to block anyone or get in the way while the team was clearly doing their best. So I slowed down, took what I needed, and got moving again.

No frustration. Just adjustment.

## The Course and the Crowd

The course itself was a simple out-and-back. The event also included a 10K, which meant those runners were doing two laps. One of my favorite parts of races like this is watching the front runners already heading back while most of us are still grinding forward.

I like trying to keep track of how many of the leaders are running the 10K so I can see them again on their second lap. It's humbling in the best way.

Around the 2.5-mile mark, I took a couple of needed walk breaks and then tried to push hard over the final stretch. I had enough left to lean into the last 400 meters and give what I could.

About 100 yards from the finish, one of the 10K runners flew past me as she crossed the line—finishing 10K as I was finishing my 5K. I recognized her from earlier in the race.

I wasn't upset. Mostly, I was in awe.

The speed, the efficiency, the level they're operating at—it's impressive. I may never run a 10K in under 35 minutes, but moments like that remind me how much room there is to grow. And that's motivating, not discouraging.

## Looking Ahead

More than anything, my head felt right today. Excited. Grounded. Ready to start the year with intention.

I want to keep this going. That means finding a regular training plan, spreading the mileage intelligently, and building week by week instead of forcing progress. I already have at least one 5K scheduled each month through April, and I might stretch one of them into a 10K.

For now, I'm just thankful—to be healthy enough to run, to take that first real step of the year, and to start stacking miles again.

Forward is forward. And this felt like a good place to begin.
